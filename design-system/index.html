01 Principles

Six rules that make it Boiler Room

Light and warm

Butter paper, sky steam, deep blue pipes. Never a dark page. Dark is only for the customer's own video.

Pixel art, readable words

The mascot, icons, frames and big numbers are pixels. Every word is set in a smooth, readable face.

Whole pixels, hard shadows

Outlines, notches and shadows are whole pixels, 3px each. No rounded corners, no blur, no smooth gradients.

Moves in frames

Animation steps at 12 frames a second, like a sprite. It is a function of the frame, the same rule the video renderer follows.

Cute, but honest

Every number on screen shows its source. The tone is friendly; the claims are checked.

Founder to founder

Short, plain and a little warm. Talk about launch day, not AI. Show who on the crew did what, and when you need to step in.

02 Logo

A little boiler with a play button for a belly

The mark is a 24 by 24 pixel boiler: steam on top, a valve on the side, a screen on its belly. The wordmark is drawn on the same grid. Both are generated by pixel-art.mjs.

Sizes: whole multiples only

The mark is 24 pixels square, so draw it at 24, 48, 72 or 96px. In-between sizes smear the pixels. Keep one mark-width of clear space around it.

05 Pixel rules

One pixel, 3px, sets every edge

Frames are a notched outline plus a stepped hard shadow, built from box-shadow so any element can wear one. Change --br-px and every outline, notch and shadow follows.

Notched frame, hard shadow

Colour in hard steps

Rounded corners, soft shadow

Smooth gradients, pills

Frame sizes

2px for badges and fields, 3px by default, 4px for hero moments. Never mix two sizes in one component.

Dithered edges

Sections meet in a 12px band: solid, checker, sparse. It replaces a fade.

07 Icons

Twelve by twelve, one colour

Every icon is drawn on a 12 pixel grid in currentColor, so it takes the text colour. Use them at 12, 24 or 36px. Add one by drawing its grid in pixel-art.mjs.

08 Motion

Frames, not curves

One frame is 83ms, 12 a second. Animations use steps(), never an ease. Everything stops when the viewer asks for reduced motion.
